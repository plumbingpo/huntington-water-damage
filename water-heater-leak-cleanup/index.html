Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Huntington

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

In Huntington, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceElectric units get shut down at the breaker. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • A temperature and pressure relief valve that opens or fails and discharges water.
  • Rust at the connections above the tank, either the flex lines or the inlet and outlet fittings.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
  • The tank rusting from within after its anode rod has worn away.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Stand and Subfloor Damage

Under the tank, the wood stand or the subfloor absorbs the largest share of the leak. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.

Adjacent Room Flooring

Carpet, hardwood or laminate in the hallway gets wet as water escapes below the closet door. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Stored Belongings

Boxes, furniture and keepsakes stored near a garage or closet heater sit in the water first. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

Shared Garage Wall

Water crossing a garage slab soaks the bottom plate and drywall of the wall shared with the house, then moves into the flooring on the living side.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Extract the Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

We go over the wall bases, the platform, any ceiling underneath and the adjoining rooms with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which shows how far the wet area extends.

3

Remove Wet Material

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Our crew logs the readings and lets you know as soon as the area is ready for a replacement heater.
